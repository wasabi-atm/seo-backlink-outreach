## Rules
- Open with "Hey {firstName}," then the article, linked by its title in markdown: [articleTitle](articleUrl).
- Then one or two specific things from articleHook. These were read from the live page on 24 Sep 2026. Keep the exact quoted words where the hook quotes them. Do not add facts that are not in articleHook or linkPlacement.
- State the free offer in one line and tie it to the page.
  - Segment A (offer "Founder's quote swap"): both founders give a quote for each other's article. For example: "Happy to swap founder quotes: I'll give you one for this piece, and you give one for ours."
  - Offer "Founder's quote swap only (sells video)": the company sells video or makes AI video tools, so never offer to make a video, animation or graphic. Say it plainly as context, for example "We're both in the video space, so a quote swap could work well for both of us."
  - Segment B: pick ONE of these to fit the article and the hook: a short explainer video for the post (when the post explains a process or recommends video), a blog graphic or infographic (when the post recommends visuals, lists steps or cites stats), a founder's quote (thought-leadership posts), or MTA internal data they can cite (posts about video or marketing results). If you offer internal data, keep it generic ("figures from our client projects") and never invent numbers.
- Then: "In return, just a credit link back to [motiontheagency.com](http://motiontheagency.com), or we'll pay for placement instead." For a quote swap, the link-back is mutual, so phrase it to fit.
- End with "Would you be open to exploring this?" then "Cheers," and "Sam".
- Under 110 words in the body, not counting the sign-off. British spelling (optimise, colour). No em dashes or en dashes. Never ask for a call or meeting. Plain, friendly, no flattery, no "I hope this finds you well".
- The subject is short and lower case, and names their article, e.g. "your content pillars piece" or "the onboarding videos post".
- Tone for hooks that point out mistakes: helpful, not smug. For example: "small thing, but..."
- If fit is "ok" or the hook is thin, lean on the offer being useful for that section (named from linkPlacement).
